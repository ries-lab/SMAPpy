# uiPSF in SMAPpy: what the blur σ and pupil magnitude are

20 October 2026 · Jonas Ries (written with Claude from five study sessions)

The pupil model now runs inside SMAPpy and its spline matches the fitter to under a nanometre, but the model still does not fit bead data at the noise: it absorbs the mismatch into an extra blur σ of 40–105 nm and a pupil magnitude that falls towards the rim. The magnitude is mostly explained by less high-angle (supercritical) light than the model assumes; the blur is microscope-specific and its cause is still open.

## Where the integration stands

The pupil model is usable for calibration in SMAPpy today; what it learns about the microscope still needs interpreting.

- **Built, on main:** the *PSF calibration* window with two methods (bead average, pupil model), start from a previous model, save model, fit quality by Spline 3D, an *Install PyTorch* button, and bead picking that ignores faint specks. uipsf reads data only through SMAPpy; the old v1 bridge is gone.
- **Verified (M6):** through the whole chain the z slope is 0.9986 and the photons are within 0.2 % of the bead average's. Spline 3D and uipsf's own localisation agree to 0.6 nm rms on Zenodo (CRLB 2.5 nm), after fixing a spline export that counted the pixel twice.
- **Two channels:** fit quality now uses Spline 3D 2C's global fit; Zenodo 2ch gives 4.5 nm median error and a photon ratio of 0.837 against the learnt 0.838.
- **Not yet:** the microscope database (optics are typed in), retiring *Bead calibration*, in situ learning (M5).
- **One loose end:** against the stage positions both localisers find a z slope of 1.011 on Zenodo 1ch, so it comes from the model or the stage, not the spline.

## The blur σ

Every dataset needs an extra Gaussian blur beyond the pixel, and its size depends on the microscope: 40–83 nm on M2, 0–32 nm on M5, with no overlap. σ here has the pixel's own share taken out (uipsf learns at pixel centres, so the learnt σ contains the pixel width, √(σ² + p²/12)); CRLBs are below 1 nm, so every spread below is real.

| dataset | camera | beads used | σx, σy beyond the pixel (nm) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zenodo 1ch, 40 nm beads | Evolve 512 EMCCD | 59 | 56, 63 |
| Zenodo 2ch, main channel | Evolve 512 EMCCD | 70 pairs | 68, 49 |
| Zenodo 2ch, secondary channel | same chip | 70 pairs | 48, 65 |
| Zenodo FD, field centre | Prime 95B sCMOS | 30 | 61, 85 |
| Zenodo FD, whole field | Prime 95B sCMOS | 30 | 77, 105 |
| Lab M2, June 2026 | Evolve 512 EMCCD | 14 | 68, 64 |
| Lab M2, Aug–Oct 2026 (4 runs) | Andor iXon EMCCD | 11–22 | 53–83, 40–57 |
| Lab M5, Feb–Sep 2026 (4 runs) | Hamamatsu Fusion BT sCMOS | 8–22 | 1–32, 0–6 |

- **Microscope-specific, not camera-type-specific:** the FD sCMOS blurs as much as the EMCCDs, M5's sCMOS hardly at all.
- **Anisotropic:** larger along x on M2's iXon and on M5; M2's Evolve run is isotropic, but it predates July's camera swap and realignment.
- **The two 2ch channels share a chip but are widened along different axes**, so part of σ comes from each channel's own light path after the splitter.
- **Varies day to day by up to 30 nm** on one microscope.
- **Grows over the field** (FD: 85 → 105 nm, deviance 1.8 → 5.7).

## The pupil magnitude

The learnt magnitude is mostly radial — bright in the centre, falling to the rim — on every dataset, and most of it is explained by less high-angle light than the model assumes.

- **Shape:** radial RMS 0.10–0.18 on M2, 0.22–0.32 on M5 (about twice); the non-symmetric part is small (0.03–0.12). That is the pattern simulated errors in the NA or the aperture edge leave, not a missing phase aberration.
- **What explains it:** on M2 (Zenodo 1ch and 2ch) the data want about half the modelled supercritical light — a transmission of 0.5–0.6 above the critical angle, or an effective NA of 1.40–1.42 instead of 1.43. Either gains 4 350 (1ch) or 41 000 nats (2ch) and roughly halves the magnitude. The data cannot tell NA from transmission: both thin the pupil's edge.
- **Plausible in size:** Shavit et al. 2025 found objectives of the same nominal NA collecting about 2× different supercritical light, and measured NA ≈ 1.43 on objectives sold as 1.45.
- **It also makes the z scale:** a missing magnitude asks for a z scale of 0.93 on simulated beads that have none; on Zenodo the asked-for scale goes 0.92 → 0.96 → 0.98 as the magnitude is fixed, free, free at order 8. All lab stacks ask for 0.89–0.98, most for M5 (NA 1.50 under oil 1.518).
- **Fixing it costs:** holding the magnitude uniform lowers σ by up to 11 nm but raises the deviance per pixel by 0.4–1.5 and the z bias to 21–30 nm, the worst choice measured.

## What causes σ and the magnitude: what was tested

Of twelve candidate causes, only the effective NA / supercritical transmission explains a large effect (the magnitude); nothing tested explains more than ~30 nm of the 50–100 nm blur.

| candidate cause | how it was tested | effect on σ | effect on the magnitude | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Effective NA below nominal, or less supercritical light | NA and transmission scanned on Zenodo 1ch, 2ch, FD | ≤ 1 nm | halves it; +4 350 / +41 000 nats | **explains most of the magnitude**; NA and transmission not separable |
| Pixel sampled at its centre instead of integrated | synthetic point source, pixel integrated 16 × 16 | exactly the pixel's share, √(p²/12) | none | accounted for: the share is taken out above |
| Stage wander between z steps | shared drift per slice; drift per bead | 2–4 nm; per bead none | none | ruled out (2–5 nm per slice) |
| Missing pupil freedom | a free pixel-wise pupil (8 192 values) | returns to (65, 73) nm; fixing σ costs 228 000 nats | — | ruled out: σ is not a stand-in for pupil detail |
| Stage z scale | z scale freed (probe) | ≤ 1 nm (one M5 run 8 nm) | absorbs 23–46 % of the radial part | the z scale is mostly the magnitude, not the stage |
| Polychromatic emission | Chromatix model, 40–80 nm emission band | +5–11 nm | — | small part of σ |
| Field-dependent aberrations | synthetic, 0.1–0.2 rad varying per bead | +10–30 nm | hardly | part of σ over a large field (FD); too much deviance to be the main cause |
| Bead shape and its height on the coverslip | shell vs solid bead; supercritical light fading over a 40 nm bead | +3 nm; ≤ 1.4 nm | — | ruled out for 40 nm beads |
| Wrong sample index (water) | n_med varied on the 40 nm sets | small | — | ruled out as the main cause |
| Cylindrical lens outside the pupil plane | paraxial theory; pixel sizes compared by likelihood | none: the Evolve behind the same lens is round | none: the lens adds a pure quadratic phase | ruled out; its squeeze is the non-square pixel, which is the right model |
| Camera type (EMCCD vs sCMOS) | comparison across datasets | FD sCMOS 61–105 nm, M5 sCMOS 0–32 nm | — | not the camera type alone |
| Zernike order 6 vs 8 | held-out comparison | — | z scale asked 0.96 → 0.98 | order 8 fits better; keep it |

### The cylindrical lens outside the pupil plane (21 October 2026)

M2's 1 m cylindrical lens sits about halfway between a focusing lens and an image plane that is relayed to the camera, not in a plane conjugate to the back focal plane. This squeezes the image along the lens's power axis, which the model represents as non-square pixels (116 × 127 nm, measured by moving a bead with a calibrated stage). The question was whether that representation is right, and whether the lens causes the anisotropic σ or the magnitude. Nothing was refitted; this is theory plus existing results.

- **The model is the right description of the lens.** The image-side NA is about 0.01, so paraxial optics applies. A thin lens anywhere between pupil and camera then changes the camera image in exactly two ways: a quadratic phase in the pupil (astigmatism plus defocus) and a scale along its axis. That scale applies to the PSF's shape and the beads' positions alike. So the squeeze is a non-square pixel for the PSF too, and "the positions squeezed but not the PSF" is not a physical case. A relay that magnifies x and y equally keeps the ratio. Because the objective is telecentric on the sample side, the squeeze does not depend on z.
- **The data agree.** The bead positions are free in a calibration, so the pixel size acts only on the PSF's shape. The likelihood still picks the stage's 116 × 127 nm (127 × 116 on the iXon, which is turned 90°) over square 127 nm, by 12 000 and 41 000 nats.
- **The sign fits a positive lens:** the power axis is the 127 nm axis on both cameras. The Evolve learns Noll 6 = −0.67 rad and the iXon +0.55, as a converging cylinder predicts for each orientation.
- **The squeeze fits; the size of the astigmatism needs the geometry.** With f = 1 m, a 9.5 % squeeze needs the lens about 95–190 mm before the image plane, which is consistent with "halfway". The astigmatism c = πr²/(2√6 λf) needs a beam about 1.5 mm wide at the lens (r ≈ 0.76 mm). An unmagnified 100×/1.43 pupil, halved at halfway, would give about 1.9 rad instead of 0.55. Either the pupil is reduced before the focusing lens, or the lens sits nearer the image: to be checked against the bench.
- **The lens does not cause the σ asymmetry or the magnitude.** On the iXon the extra blur lies along the power axis (91 against 66 nm). Behind the same lens the Evolve's σ is round (77, 74), and M5's sCMOS is anisotropic without it. A weak lens with a 1.5 mm beam adds no blur. The magnitude is radial on every dataset, which the lens's pure quadratic phase does not produce. The iXon itself, which smears charge along its readout axis, and July's realignment remain the candidates.
- **The magnitude does not hold the asymmetry either** (Zenodo, from the model-choices runs):
  - A symmetric magnitude keeps σ as anisotropic as before: 1ch (64, 75) nm against (66, 73) with the magnitude free.
  - σ held round, at 0–40 nm, leaves the astigmatic magnitude modes (Noll 5, 6) unchanged. The asymmetry goes into the phase astigmatism instead (Noll 6 −0.014 / −0.024 rad, 55–59 CRLBs) and into a four-fold magnitude mode (Noll 14 +0.03 / +0.04).
  - A free round σ, and any of this on the lab stacks, has not been fitted.

**Not tested yet:** charge-transfer smear in EMCCDs (fast vertical shift, clock voltage; a one-sided trail along the readout axes, from the literature); a dichroic's s/p transmission (a candidate for σx ≠ σy); the polystyrene bead's own index (1.59) near the coverslip for 100 nm beads; where in the spot a blur-free model misses (core, wings or defocused rings); the FD camera's DESPECKLE filter.

## Model choices: what the measurements recommend

Most options in the window's *model* section can go: one calibration per choice on the same beads, judged on 20 % held-out beads and by Spline 3D, on Zenodo 1ch, 2ch and simulated beads. Nothing is changed yet; these are proposals.

| choice | recommendation | measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zernike order | default 8 (now 6), field under *more* | held out −10 000 / −69 000 nats, z bias 5.4 → 3.4 and 3.3 → 2.6 nm, same time |
| Pupil magnitude | free only; drop *fixed* and *symmetric* | fixed: z bias 21–30 nm on every dataset; symmetric loses 29 000 / 216 000 nats |
| Blur | drop the choice, σ always free | fixing it at 0, 20 or 40 nm costs 59 000–660 000 nats, z bias 12–20 nm |
| z scale | keep fixed at 1, not in the form | it is mostly the magnitude, not the stage |
| Photons per slice (hidden) | bleaching of order 2, after the first fit | −27 000 / −29 000 nats held out, z bias 3.9 / 2.2 nm |
| Stage drift (hidden) | shared drift, freed after the main fit; per file to do | +4 800 / +53 300 nats, z bias 2.9 / 1.9 nm |
| First stage (hidden) | keep order 4 | order 2 is a trap on 2ch |
| Slice thinning (hidden) | 100 nm instead of 50 | same fit, 1.6× faster on 2ch |

With these, the *model* section shrinks to *bead diameter*, with *Zernike order*, *rejection (k)* and *held out* under *more*.

## Open questions and next steps

The blur's cause is the main open question; the cheapest decisive steps need an hour at the microscope.

1. **Decide the model choices** above (your call), then retire *Bead calibration*.
2. **Where does a blur-free model miss?** Residuals of a fit with σ = 0 by z and by radius in the spot (core, wings, defocused rings) on Zenodo and the lab stacks. No new data needed.
3. **Read the existing lab results for a transfer trail:** is the atlas's signed residual one-sided along the camera's readout axes, and does σ follow the Andor's vertical shift speed, clock voltage and readout rate in the metadata? No new data needed.
4. **At the microscope, M2's Andor (about half an hour):** beads at one position with two or three vertical shift speeds and clock voltages, EM on and off, two readout rates. If σ follows the clocks it is charge transfer; if neither, it is the optics.
5. **At the microscope, to settle the magnitude:** image the objective's back focal plane — an emitter in index-matched medium gives the true NA and transmission, a dye layer in water the supercritical light — or take beads in index-matched medium to separate NA from transmission.
6. **Bead size and medium:** 20/40/100/200 nm beads in water, index-matched and dried, to see what the bead itself adds.
7. **Then model it:** a radial magnitude with a soft edge (an effective NA plus a transmission) instead of free Zernikes; field dependence for large fields; a drift per stack and file.

**Also found on the way:** on 2ch, learning stalls with the window's default indices (n_imm 1.518, n_med 1.33) and converges with v1's 1.516 / 1.35 — since understood and fixed: not the indices but the start's backgrounds and the solver (architecture.md, "Why Zenodo 2ch stalled"). M2's camera changed in July (Evolve → Andor iXon, turned 90°, realigned), which the camera database files under M1; no file records read noise, the objective, dichroic or 3D lens.
